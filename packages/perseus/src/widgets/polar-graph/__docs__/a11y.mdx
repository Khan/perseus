# Polar Graph Accessibility

The Polar Graph widget reuses Interactive Graph's movable point, drag hook
and hitbox layer, and wraps them in the same figure structure, so keyboard
and screen-reader behavior match for the parts the two widgets share.

## Keyboard Navigation

-   Each point is a focusable button in the tab order, in the order the points
    are numbered.
-   `Up` and `Down` move the focused point one grid circle outward or inward
    along its spoke. Movement stops at the outer circle and at the pole.
-   `Left` and `Right` turn the focused point one spoke counterclockwise or
    clockwise.
-   Dragging with a mouse or finger snaps to the nearest circle and spoke, and
    gives the dragged point focus so keyboard adjustments can follow.

## Screen Reader Support

-   The graph is a `figure` named by the author's `fullGraphAriaLabel`, or
    "A polar graph." when none is set.
-   The figure is a tab stop, as on interactive graph, so a screen reader
    user hears the instructions before reaching the first handle.
-   Its description reads the keyboard instructions first, then the author's
    `fullGraphAriaDescription`, then a live list of every point.
-   Each handle announces its role and position in polar form: "Point 1 at
    radius 3, angle 45 degrees." or "Far tip of the limaçon at radius 4, angle
    0 degrees." In radians the angle is a fraction of π, such as "π/4".
-   The group holding the figure names the sub-type, for example "A rose
    curve with k equal to 3 on a polar graph." The rose's k is changed with
    two labeled buttons under the graph, and the new value is announced.
    When the author fixes k, the buttons and the sentence about them in
    the instructions are left out; the value still shows. The point
    sub-type has no group name, as on interactive graph: each point names
    itself and the figure already says "polar graph".
-   For an angle the group name carries the measure, "An angle at the pole
    measuring 5π/6 radians.", and updates as either side moves. The two
    handles are named "Initial side of the angle" and "Terminal side of the
    angle".
-   The grid lines and labels are hidden from screen readers because the
    figure and the points already describe them.
-   Author-drawn locked figures follow interactive graph: each is an image
    named by the author's aria label, and a figure with no label is hidden
    from screen readers. The editor can generate a label from the figure's
    name, measurements and appearance. Authors should also describe locked
    figures in the graph description.
-   TeX labels on the graph are hidden from screen readers, as on interactive
    graph; a figure's labels are spoken as part of its generated aria label.
-   When the author labels points, the label becomes the point's name, "Point
    A at radius 3, angle 0 degrees." The visible label is hidden from screen
    readers so it is not read twice, and a static graph does not draw it, as
    on interactive graph.
-   A graded graph the learner must answer ends its instructions with
    "Required", as interactive graph does.
-   An ungraded graph reads "Use this graph to check your thinking, but it
    does not count as your answer." before everything else, the same sentence
    interactive graph uses.

## Visible Labels and Markings

-   Circles are labeled with their radius along the polar axis.
-   Spokes are labeled with their angle, in degrees or radians, every 30° or
    at every spoke when spokes are further apart.
-   A focused point shows the same focus ring as Interactive Graph.

## Best Practices

-   Always provide a `fullGraphAriaLabel` that says what the graph is for.
-   Keep spokes at least 15° apart on small screens so snap targets stay
    larger than a fingertip.
